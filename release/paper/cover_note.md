## Summary of the contribution (for a cover letter)

The kingdoms of early medieval Britain have been gone for more than a thousand years, yet their names are used by police forces, utilities, regiments and some 254 businesses and voluntary bodies in OpenStreetMap. This paper measures, across the whole UK, where those names are used. Reading every OpenStreetMap feature named after twelve kingdoms and comparing their locations with approximate kingdom extents fixed in an analysis plan beforehand, it finds that businesses and voluntary bodies named after Wessex, Mercia and Northumbria are concentrated inside their kingdoms at 44 times the per-resident rate outside, a result that survives every planned check and is absent in a placebo with the wrong kingdoms. Names that leave mostly stop just over the boundary. Public bodies are no more local than businesses, but street names, chosen by developers and councils, travel much further. The study connects regional identity, commercial naming and historical geography, with open data and code.
